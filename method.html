The dataset

One government file decides
what Swiss insurance pays for.

It is called the Spezialitätenliste. If a medicine is on it, insurance covers most of the cost. If it is not, the patient pays the whole price. The file is public and updated every month, but almost impossible to read in its raw form.

The list

10,405 medicines
updated every month
on the list →
insurance pays 90%
patient pays 10%
not on it →
patient pays all of it

Nine million people are covered by this one file. Almost nobody outside the system reads it.

What I found

Three things came out of it.

Corrected after publishing

I checked my own headline number. It was wrong by a factor of five.

what I first published

10.6%
comparing like with like
264 pairs

My first version asked how much cheaper a generic is than the original. It matched products by drug class alone, so a box of 100 tablets could be compared against a box of 10. That is not a saving, it is a smaller box.

  • Same data, same formula. The only change: both packs have to hold the same amount of medicine.
  • 80% of the pairs I first counted were comparing different quantities.
  • 104 of them matched an injection to a tablet.
What this cannot show

264 pairs is a small slice of the list, not a random sample. And list prices are not what the system actually spends.

Analysis

One percent of the products carry most of the price.

When the average is thirty-five times the typical price, the average has stopped describing anything real. The list is really two lists: thousands of cheap everyday medicines, and a small group of very expensive ones where nearly all the money sits.

Analysis

Expensive medicines come with more conditions attached.

  • Costlier drug classes carry more prescribing restrictions. ρ = 0.56, but with only 14 classes the plausible range runs from 0.04 to 0.84. A pattern, not a finding.
  • Price does not predict whether a generic exists. ρ = −0.19, p = 0.51. I expected the opposite, and reported it anyway.
  • Pack sizes read out of German free text on 10,403 of 10,405 records.
What this cannot show

The list records prices, not how much of each medicine is actually used. Public prices also leave out the confidential discounts negotiated behind them.

How I work

The same five steps every month.

The file changes monthly, so the process has to be repeatable and it has to fail loudly when something is wrong.

Download

One folder per month. Nothing gets overwritten.

Read

If a record will not parse, the code stops rather than guesses.

Check

A price that jumped overnight is a bug, not a policy change.

Analyse

Write down what the data cannot show before writing what it does.

Try to break it

Attack my own headline number. This is where one of them fell over.
